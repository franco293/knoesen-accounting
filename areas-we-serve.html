Nelson Mandela Bay & Beyond

An accountant for Nelson Mandela Bay

Based in Walmer Heights, Gqeberha. Close enough to meet over coffee, set up to work with you wherever you are.

The practice is based in Walmer Heights, Gqeberha — the city many people still call Port Elizabeth. Nelson Mandela Bay is a metro of owner-run businesses: automotive suppliers feeding the plants in Struandale and Kariega, guesthouses and restaurants along the beachfront, trades and construction firms working across the western suburbs, professional practices in Newton Park and Mill Park, and a steady flow of new companies registered every month.

What they mostly have in common is that the owner is also the finance department. That is who this practice is built for.

Elsewhere in South Africa

SARS eFiling, CIPC and the UIF are all national systems, and every submission we make is electronic. Several clients have never been to our office. If you are outside the Eastern Cape and would rather deal with a small practice than a call centre, distance is not the obstacle people assume it is.

What does not travel well is the informal side — dropping in with a box of slips, or a face-to-face conversation about a difficult year. If those matter to you, being in the metro genuinely helps.

Local Detail

Things that come up specifically around here

Automotive supply chains

Suppliers into the Kariega and Struandale plants tend to run tight margins on long payment terms. Cash-flow reporting and accurate management accounts matter far more here than an annual set of statements delivered eight months late.

Seasonal tourism trade

Guesthouses, restaurants and operators along the beachfront and in Jeffreys Bay earn most of their year between December and Easter. Provisional tax estimates built on a flat twelve-month assumption consistently get these businesses wrong.

Trades and construction

Retentions, progress billing and subcontractor payments make revenue recognition genuinely tricky, and getting VAT timing wrong on a progress payment is an easy and expensive mistake.

NPOs, schools and churches

Non-profits carry their own reporting expectations from funders and the Department of Social Development, alongside SARS obligations that do not disappear simply because an organisation is not trading for profit.
