Use this checklist to prepare for a monthly bookkeeping discussion or organise records for an agreed engagement. It is a practical starting point for South African SMEs, not a complete statutory checklist or a substitute for advice about your business.

1. Set the period and sharing arrangements

  • Choose the month and business. Keep each entity's records separate. If work is behind, identify the last completed reconciliation rather than assuming the previous month is finished.
  • List the accounts involved. Include business bank accounts, cards, cash, payment providers and loans that need to be considered.
  • Agree who does what. Confirm who supplies records, answers transaction questions, approves payroll and receives reports, and when those inputs are needed.
  • Agree how to share documents. Ask which transfer method and access permissions are appropriate before sending statements, tax documents or employee information. Do not share banking or eFiling passwords or one-time codes.

A first enquiry only needs a description of your business and the help you need. The detailed records below belong in the arrangements agreed for the work, not in a public comment or an unsolicited attachment.

2. Gather the monthly records

Bank, card and payment-provider records

  • Statements covering the full period for each relevant account, including an account with no activity.
  • Transaction exports if the agreed accounting process needs them, with the period and account clearly identified.
  • Payment-provider settlement reports showing sales, refunds, fees and the amount paid into the bank. A net settlement is not necessarily the same as total sales.
  • Explanations for transfers between your own accounts, owner contributions, drawings and loan movements so they are not mistaken for sales or expenses.

Sales and customer balances

  • Sales invoices, credit notes, refunds and cash-sale or till summaries, where applicable.
  • A list of customer amounts still unpaid, including disputed invoices or receipts that have not been matched to an invoice.
  • Notes about deposits, cancelled work or other unusual transactions that need their treatment checked.

Purchases and supplier balances

  • Supplier invoices, receipts and credit notes, including expenses paid personally on behalf of the business.
  • Supplier statements and bills not yet paid. A payment leaving the bank does not by itself explain what was bought or whether VAT can be deducted.
  • A short business-purpose note for an unclear expense, and a list of missing documents to request from suppliers.

If your business is VAT-registered, check the supporting-document requirements separately. Keep the relevant invoices and any permitted alternative evidence; do not assume every bank payment includes deductible VAT. See SARS's tax-invoice guidance.

Cash, stock, assets and other changes

  • A petty-cash record with supporting vouchers and a cash count, if the business handles cash.
  • Stock counts or movement reports where relevant, with notes on damaged, missing or obsolete stock.
  • Documents for equipment purchases or disposals, new finance agreements, and changes to existing loans or leases.
  • Information about significant changes, such as a new bank account, premises, owner funding or a business activity that was not part of the previous month.

Keep the underlying records and their explanations even after a transaction has been entered into software. Ask about the retention requirements that apply to your entity, tax position and any unresolved audit or dispute; completing month-end is not a reason to delete the supporting documents.

3. Keep payroll information separate

Where payroll is included in the engagement, agree a separate way to provide and approve the information. Payroll preparation may need to happen before payday, so do not wait for this month-end check to raise a change.

  • Identify changes in employee count, pay frequency, starters and leavers.
  • Provide approved hours, overtime, commission, leave and other pay changes through the agreed process.
  • Make the relevant payroll reports, payment records and employer declarations available to the people responsible for reconciling them.
  • Keep employee names, ID numbers, salary details and bank information out of a general quote enquiry. Limit detailed records to the people who need them for the work.

For an initial payroll discussion, the approximate employee count, pay frequency, current system and help needed are enough to start. Our payroll service and payroll compliance guide explain the wider scope.

4. List the questions that prevent completion

Keep a short list of unresolved items rather than hiding them in several message threads. Record the transaction reference, the question, who can answer it and whether it has been resolved. Mark missing information as missing; do not invent a description to make the books appear complete.

5. Review the figures and next actions

The reports and checks depend on the agreed engagement. Useful questions for the owner and accountant include:

  • Do the reconciled bank and card balances agree with the statements, and are differences explained?
  • Which customer invoices need collection and which supplier bills need attention?
  • Are unusual income, expense, asset or loan movements understood?
  • What information is still missing, and how does that limit the reports or submissions that can be completed?
  • Which payroll, VAT, tax or other deadlines apply next, and who is responsible for each?

Use the South African tax deadline calendar as a reference, then confirm the dates relevant to your business. A completed bookkeeping checklist does not prove that every return has been submitted, accepted or paid.

If you need year-end statements as well, discuss them separately. Our annual financial statements service provides compilations only, with an individual quote; it does not include an audit or independent review.

6. Prepare an enquiry for recurring support

You do not need to send a full month's records to ask about monthly bookkeeping support. Describe:

  • What the business does and its entity type.
  • The accounting system or spreadsheets you use, approximate monthly transaction volume and number of accounts.
  • Whether the books are current or which periods need catch-up work.
  • Whether you need payroll, VAT or other tax work alongside bookkeeping, and any deadline you are working towards.
  • The reports you need to run the business and your preferred contact method.
