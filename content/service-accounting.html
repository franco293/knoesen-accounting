Questions

Accounting questions we get asked

What is the difference between an audit, a review and a compilation?

A compilation means the accountant prepares the annual financial statements from your records without expressing an assurance opinion. An independent review and an audit are assurance engagements with progressively more testing. The required engagement depends on the applicable law, the company's circumstances and its Memorandum of Incorporation. We provide compilations only; an audit or independent review requires a suitable assurance provider.

How often should bookkeeping be done?

Monthly, in almost every case. Monthly bookkeeping keeps VAT returns accurate, makes payroll reconciliations straightforward, and means year-end is a review rather than a scramble. It also means you can see how the business is actually performing while you can still do something about it.

How long must I keep my accounting records?

Generally seven years. The Companies Act requires company records to be kept for seven years, and the Tax Administration Act requires records supporting a return to be retained for five years from the date the return was submitted — longer if an audit, objection or appeal is in progress.
