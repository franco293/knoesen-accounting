Bookkeeping that stays current

Most accounting problems are actually bookkeeping problems wearing a disguise. A VAT return that cannot be trusted, a set of annual financial statements that takes four months to produce, a SARS verification nobody can answer — all of it usually traces back to records that were never kept properly in the first place.

We record your monthly transactions accurately and keep them up to date: bank and credit card reconciliations, supplier and customer ledgers, cash and petty cash, and the supporting documents filed where they can actually be found again. Done monthly, it is routine. Done annually, it is archaeology.

Management accounts you can use

Annual financial statements tell you how last year went, which is useful for SARS and largely useless for running a business. Management accounts tell you how this month went, while you can still change something.

We prepare management accounts at whatever interval genuinely helps — monthly for businesses with tight cash flow or bank covenants, quarterly for steadier operations — with a short plain-language note on what moved and why, rather than a stack of unexplained figures.

Annual financial statements

We compile annual financial statements for companies, close corporations, sole proprietors, partnerships, trusts, non-profit organisations and other entities, prepared on an appropriate reporting framework — typically IFRS for SMEs for owner-managed companies.

These are the statements your bank asks for when you apply for finance, that a funder or landlord will want to see, that support your company's income tax return, and that a buyer will scrutinise if you ever sell. They are worth getting right.

Compilation, review or audit — which do you need?

A compilation means the accountant prepares the statements from your records without expressing an assurance opinion. An independent review and an audit are assurance engagements with progressively more testing and cost.

Which applies to your company depends on its Public Interest Score, who compiles the statements, and what its Memorandum of Incorporation says. Most owner-managed South African small companies need only a compilation. If yours needs an audit, we will tell you plainly — an audit requires a registered auditor, which is a different registration from ours.

Accounting questions we get asked

What is the difference between an audit, a review and a compilation?

A compilation means the accountant prepares the annual financial statements from your records without expressing an assurance opinion. An independent review and an audit are assurance engagements with progressively more testing. Which one your company needs depends on its Public Interest Score and its Memorandum of Incorporation. Most owner-managed small companies in South Africa require only a compilation.

How often should bookkeeping be done?

Monthly, in almost every case. Monthly bookkeeping keeps VAT returns accurate, makes payroll reconciliations straightforward, and means year-end is a review rather than a scramble. It also means you can see how the business is actually performing while you can still do something about it.

Do you work with Xero, Sage, QuickBooks or spreadsheets?

We work with whatever you already use, including well-kept spreadsheets. If your current system is causing more problems than it solves, we will say so and help you move — but changing software is never a precondition for working together.

My books are years behind. Is that a problem?

It is common and it is fixable. We reconstruct the records period by period, bring the filings up to date, and then set up a routine so it does not happen again. The Companies Act requires accounting records to be retained for seven years, so there is usually more to work with than owners expect.

How long must I keep my accounting records?

Generally seven years. The Companies Act requires company records to be kept for seven years, and the Tax Administration Act requires records supporting a return to be retained for five years from the date the return was submitted — longer if an audit, objection or appeal is in progress.
