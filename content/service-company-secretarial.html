The filings nobody remembers until it is too late

CIPC obligations have a particular way of going wrong: nothing happens for a long time, and then everything happens at once. A missed annual return does not generate an immediate penalty notice — it quietly starts a deregistration process, and the first many owners hear of it is when a bank freezes an account or a tender application is rejected because the company no longer exists.

We keep the register current: annual returns filed in the window, beneficial ownership declarations lodged, and changes to directors, members, addresses and company details recorded when they happen rather than reconstructed later.

CIPC annual returns

Every company and close corporation must file an annual return with CIPC. Companies file within 30 business days after the anniversary of the date of incorporation. Close corporations file within the anniversary month of incorporation and the two months that follow. The fee is calculated on turnover.

This is not the same thing as your SARS income tax return, and filing one does nothing about the other. The two are confused constantly, and the confusion is what puts otherwise well-run companies into deregistration.

Beneficial ownership

South African companies and close corporations must file information about the natural persons who ultimately own or effectively control the entity. It is lodged with CIPC alongside the annual return, and an incomplete or outdated beneficial ownership record can block the annual return itself from being accepted — so it is no longer a formality that can be left for later.

Registrations and deregistrations

We register new companies at CIPC and handle the registrations that follow: income tax at SARS, and UIF at the Department of Employment and Labour. Our start-up checklist sets out the full sequence.

We also handle the other end. When a company or close corporation has genuinely stopped trading, deregistering it deliberately is far better than abandoning it — an abandoned entity keeps accruing obligations, and directors keep carrying duties, until it is formally wound up.

If your company is already in deregistration

It is usually recoverable. Re-instatement requires bringing the outstanding annual returns up to date and lodging a re-instatement application with supporting evidence that the company was trading or holds assets. It takes time, so the earlier it is started the better. Talk to us about it →

Questions

CIPC questions we get asked

What happens if I do not file my CIPC annual return?

CIPC may place the company in deregistration process and ultimately deregister it. A deregistered company loses its legal existence: its bank accounts can be frozen, it cannot contract lawfully, and directors lose the protection of separate legal personality. Reinstatement is possible but slower and more expensive than simply filing on time.

When is my CIPC annual return due?

Companies must file within 30 business days after the anniversary of their date of incorporation. Close corporations file within the anniversary month of incorporation and the two months following. The fee is based on turnover, and it is separate from your SARS income tax return — the two are often confused.

What is a beneficial ownership declaration?

South African companies and close corporations must file information about the natural persons who ultimately own or effectively control the entity. It is filed with CIPC alongside the annual return, and non-compliance can block the annual return from being accepted.

Can you register a new company for me?

Yes — company registration at CIPC, income tax registration at SARS, and UIF registration at the Department of Employment and Labour. Our start-up checklist explains what is involved and in what order.

Do I need a company, or is a sole proprietorship enough?

A sole proprietorship is simpler and cheaper to run, but gives you no separation between business and personal liability, and its profits are taxed at your personal marginal rate — which reaches 45%. A company gives limited liability and may qualify for Small Business Corporation rates starting at 0%. Which is better depends on your risk, your profit level and your plans, and it is worth ten minutes of conversation before you register anything.
