Income tax, for whatever you are

We prepare and submit income tax returns for individuals, companies, close corporations, partnerships, sole proprietors and trusts. The return itself is the easy part; the value is in what goes into it — making sure every deduction you are entitled to is claimed, and that nothing is claimed that will not survive a SARS verification.

Provisional tax

If you earn income that is not subject to PAYE — business income, rental income, significant investment income — you are almost certainly a provisional taxpayer, with estimates due twice a year and an optional third top-up payment after year-end.

Provisional tax is where careless work becomes expensive. An estimate that is too low attracts an underestimation penalty; an estimate that is too high hands SARS an interest-free loan of your working capital for months. We base estimates on actual year-to-date figures rather than last year's number with a guess on top.

When SARS comes back to you

Verification requests, additional assessments, penalties for late returns, and disputes all follow set procedures with hard deadlines. Missing an objection deadline can extinguish an otherwise good case, so the first thing to do with a SARS letter is read the date on it.

Behind on your returns?

SARS levies administrative non-compliance penalties that recur every month a return remains outstanding, charged per outstanding return. Several late years compound quickly, and the penalty is charged whether or not any tax was actually owed for those years.

Questions

Tax questions we get asked

Am I a provisional taxpayer?

Generally, you are a provisional taxpayer if you earn income that is not subject to PAYE — such as business income, rental income or significant investment income. Someone who earns only a salary usually is not. There is an exemption where income from interest, dividends, foreign dividends, rental from fixed property and remuneration from an unregistered employer totals R30 000 or less for the tax year.

What happens if I file my tax return late?

SARS charges administrative non-compliance penalties that recur every month a return remains outstanding, and interest accrues on any unpaid tax. The penalties are levied per outstanding return, so several late years compound quickly. Filing late is always better than not filing.

Can you deal with SARS on my behalf?

Yes. Once you appoint us and we are linked to your profile on SARS eFiling, we can submit returns, respond to verification requests, and handle correspondence directly with SARS on your behalf.

I received an auto-assessment. Do I need to do anything?

Only if it is wrong or incomplete. An auto-assessment uses third-party data SARS already holds from employers, banks, medical schemes and retirement funds. If you have deductions SARS does not know about — home office expenses, travel claimed against a travel allowance, retirement annuity contributions or out-of-pocket medical costs — you should file a return rather than accept it. See our Filing Season 2026 guide.

What records do I need to keep for my tax return?

Records supporting a return must generally be retained for five years from the date the return was submitted, and longer where an audit, objection or appeal is in progress. In practice, keeping everything for seven years aligns your tax and Companies Act obligations.
