Why your bonus feels so heavily taxed

Almost everyone who receives a 13th cheque has the same reaction: far more tax came off it than off an ordinary month's salary. Nothing has gone wrong, and there is no penalty rate for bonuses.

Your monthly PAYE is worked out as though you earn that salary every month for a year. The rebates and the tax threshold are spread across all twelve months, so each month gets a share of them. A bonus arrives on top of a year's income that has already used up that threshold and those rebates — so every rand of it is taxed at your highest rate, with nothing left to shelter it.

That is why a R30 000 bonus for someone earning R30 000 a month is taxed much more heavily than their ordinary R30 000 salary month. The salary month carries a twelfth of the rebates; the bonus carries none.

What this calculator does and doesn't cover

It covers the ordinary case: a salary, a cash bonus or 13th cheque, the rebates for your age, the medical scheme fees tax credit, your own retirement contributions and UIF. It works the tax out the correct way — the tax on a year with the bonus less the tax on the same year without it — rather than applying a flat marginal rate, which overstates the tax whenever a bonus straddles a bracket.

It does not account for:

  • a bonus paid partly in shares, or any non-cash incentive;
  • a travel allowance or company car, both of which change the amount subject to PAYE;
  • commission that varies month to month — if most of your pay is commission, the annualised assumption here will not hold;
  • the additional medical expenses tax credit, or lump sums on retirement or retrenchment, which have their own tables.

If your employer has deducted noticeably more than this suggests, that is worth checking rather than accepting — it usually comes right on assessment, but not always. Ask us and we will look at it.
