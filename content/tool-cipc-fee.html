For a close corporation the standard fee stays the same and a R150.00 penalty is added for each late lodgment.

Two or more outstanding returns is the point at which CIPC may refer the entity for deregistration. That is a different order of problem from a fee: a deregistered company cannot legally trade, its bank accounts can be frozen, and its assets pass to the state until it is reinstated. If this is where you are, it is worth dealing with now rather than at the next renewal.

The fee is rarely the real cost

Annual return fees are small. A dormant company pays R100 a year. What makes missed returns expensive is what happens after them.

CIPC may refer a company or close corporation for deregistration once annual returns go unfiled. A deregistered entity ceases to exist as a legal person: it cannot contract, its bank accounts can be frozen, and its assets fall to the state as bona vacantia until it is reinstated. Reinstatement costs R200 in CIPC fees, but it also requires evidence the entity was trading or had assets, and every outstanding return still has to be filed and paid for.

It is also the kind of thing that surfaces at the worst moment — during a due diligence, a tender application, or when a bank asks for a current disclosure certificate.

Two Acts, two ways of charging for lateness

Companies are charged under the Companies Act, 2008. Its table gives two figures per turnover band: one for filing within 30 business days of the anniversary date, and a higher one for filing after that. The late figure replaces the standard fee.

Close corporations are charged under the Close Corporations Act, 1984. There the standard fee stays put and a flat R150 penalty is added for each late lodgment. You can tell the two apart because the CC penalty is R150 in both turnover bands, including the one whose standard fee is R4 000 — if it replaced the fee, filing late would somehow cost less than filing on time.

What this doesn't cover

  • Annual returns that fell due before 1 May 2011, which are charged on the older Companies Act, 1973 table.
  • Beneficial ownership filings, which are now a separate mandatory submission alongside the annual return and carry no fee but do carry consequences for non-filing.
  • Annual financial statements or a financial accountability supplement, which must accompany the return depending on your public interest score.
  • Any penalty SARS may raise separately — CIPC and SARS are different regulators with different deadlines.
