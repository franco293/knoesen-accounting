Why this is harder than it looks

Going from gross to net is one calculation. Going the other way is not simply that calculation reversed, because the tax on a salary depends on which bracket the salary lands in — and you do not know the bracket until you know the salary.

A common shortcut is to take the target, divide by one minus the marginal rate, and stop. That is wrong whenever the answer crosses a bracket boundary, which is exactly where people tend to be negotiating. This calculator instead searches for the gross that produces your target when put through the ordinary forward calculation, then shows you that calculation so you can check it.

Where this is genuinely useful

  • Negotiating a package. If you need R28 000 in hand, this is the number to ask for — and the "cost of the next rand" figure tells you what an extra R1 000 of take-home really costs.
  • Costing a hire. A candidate who names a take-home figure has not told you what the role costs. This converts it.
  • Comparing two offers where one includes a retirement contribution and the other does not.

Remember that the gross salary is not the full cost of employing somebody. The employer also pays UIF and, above the exemption threshold, the Skills Development Levy on top of the figure above.

What this doesn't cover

The same limits as the main PAYE calculator: no travel allowance or company car, no commission that swings month to month, no additional medical expenses credit, and no lump sums.
