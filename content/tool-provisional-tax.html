The rule that catches people out

Most provisional taxpayers learn early that the basic amount — the taxable income on your last assessment — is a safe harbour. Estimate at least that much and the under-estimation penalty cannot reach you.

That stops being true the moment your actual taxable income passes R1 million. Above that line the basic amount counts for nothing: your second estimate has to reach 80% of your actual taxable income, or SARS charges 20% of the shortfall in tax.

The people this hits hardest are the ones having their first exceptional year — a big contract, a property sale, a business that finally took off. They estimate on last year's figure exactly as they always have, and discover the safe harbour disappeared behind them. If you can see a year like that coming, that is the moment to talk to someone, not February.

How the two payments fit together

Provisional tax is not an extra tax. It is the ordinary income tax liability paid in advance, in instalments, because there is no employer withholding it for you. The first payment covers half the year's estimated tax; the second brings you up to the full estimate; and anything still short after that can be settled with a voluntary third payment to stop interest accruing.

What this calculator does and doesn't cover

It covers an individual provisional taxpayer: the brackets, the age rebates, the medical scheme fees tax credit, PAYE already withheld, the basic amount and the under-estimation penalty on both sides of the R1 million line.

It does not cover:

  • Companies and trusts. The mechanism is the same but the rates are not, and a company that qualifies as a Small Business Corporation is taxed on a different table entirely.
  • The additional medical expenses tax credit under section 6B, which depends on out-of-pocket spending this page does not ask for. If you have significant unrecovered medical costs your tax will be lower than shown.
  • The solar energy credit, foreign tax credits, or the section 11F retirement deduction — all of which reduce the figure.
  • Section 89quat interest, which runs on any underpayment where taxable income exceeds R50 000, and paragraph 27 penalties for paying late.
