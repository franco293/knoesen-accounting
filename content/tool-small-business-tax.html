Small Business Corporation status

SBC status is not something you elect — you either meet all six section 12E tests or you do not. Only one of them is visible in the numbers above, so this is a question rather than something this page can work out for you:

  • A private company, close corporation or co-operative
  • Every shareholder or member is a natural person, all year
  • No shareholder holds an interest in any other company, apart from the permitted exceptions
  • Gross income for the year is under R20 million
  • Investment income and personal-service income together are 20% or less of total receipts
  • Not a "personal service provider" as defined

Company figures include dividends tax at 20% on the after-tax profit. Turnover tax has no second layer — it replaces dividends tax as well. A sole proprietor has none either, because the money is already theirs.

Why margin decides this

Three of the four regimes tax your profit. Turnover tax taxes your revenue, and ignores your costs completely. That one difference is why the same turnover produces wildly different answers for different businesses.

A consultant turning over R900 000 with R400 000 of costs pays turnover tax of R3 000. A retailer turning over R2 000 000 on thin margins, with R100 000 of profit left, pays R30 500 — roughly a third of everything they made. Same system, opposite outcomes.

And in a bad year the asymmetry gets worse: turnover tax is payable whether or not you made a profit at all.

The company trap

A company rate can look dramatically cheaper than the individual tables until you remember that the money is in the company, not in your hand. Taking it out as a dividend costs another 20%. Tick the dividends tax box above and watch the gap close.

Which is why a company usually only wins on tax when profits stay in the business and get reinvested — or when they are drawn as salary, which puts you back on the individual tables anyway. Limited liability, credibility and access to finance are often better reasons to incorporate than the tax.

What this calculator can't tell you

It cannot tell you whether you qualify as a Small Business Corporation. Section 12E has six tests and only the R20 million gross income ceiling is visible in a turnover figure. The rest turn on who your shareholders are, whether any of them holds shares in another company, your mix of investment and personal-service income, and whether you are a personal service provider. That is why the checklist is a question and not an answer.

The shareholding condition is where most businesses fall over. An owner holding shares in a second, unrelated company — even a dormant one they had forgotten about — can disqualify an otherwise-qualifying business entirely. It is worth checking rather than assuming.

It also assumes the sole proprietor has no other income and is under 65, ignores assessed losses carried forward, the s12E accelerated wear and tear allowances an SBC can claim, and the VAT consequences of electing turnover tax.
