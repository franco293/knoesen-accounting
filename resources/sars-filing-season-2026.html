Filing Season

SARS Filing Season 2026

The dates that matter, who they apply to, and what to do if SARS auto-assessed you.

Last reviewed 3 August 2026 against SARS and National Treasury sources.

Non-provisional taxpayers 23 October 2026

Most salaried individuals. Filing opened 13 July 2026.

Provisional taxpayers 22 January 2027

Business income, rental income or significant investment income.

Trusts 22 January 2027

Trust returns opened later, on 19 September 2026.

Dates per SARS Filing Season. Reviewed 2026-08-03.

What happened before filing opened

SARS issued auto-assessment notices between 1 and 12 July 2026. If you received one, SARS has already calculated your assessment using third-party data it holds — from your employer, your bank, your medical scheme, and your retirement fund.

Filing then opened on 13 July 2026 for everyone else, and for anyone auto-assessed who needs to correct or add to what SARS produced. Trust returns opened on 19 September 2026.

Should you accept your auto-assessment?

An auto-assessment is only as complete as the data SARS received. It is generally reliable for someone whose entire financial life is visible to third parties: one salary, one medical scheme, one retirement annuity, nothing else.

It becomes unreliable the moment you have something SARS has no record of. You should file a return rather than accept the auto-assessment if any of the following apply:

  • You have a travel allowance or use a company car and keep a logbook. SARS cannot know your business kilometres, and this is frequently the largest single deduction on a return.
  • You work from home and meet the requirements for a home office deduction.
  • You paid medical expenses out of pocket that did not go through your scheme, or you or a dependant have a disability.
  • You earn rental income, freelance income, or income from a side business.
  • You donated to a registered public benefit organisation and hold a valid section 18A certificate.
  • You have foreign income or assets, or you emigrated or immigrated during the year.
  • You contributed to a retirement annuity directly, rather than through your employer's payroll.

Accepting an auto-assessment that omits a deduction is not a technicality — it means paying tax you did not owe. It is your return and your responsibility either way.

An auto-assessment refund is not confirmation that it is correct

People often treat a refund as proof the assessment was right. It is not. It only means SARS calculated that too much was withheld on the data it had. A larger refund may well have been due.

Are you a provisional taxpayer?

This determines which deadline applies to you, and people get it wrong in both directions.

Broadly, you are a provisional taxpayer if you earn income that is not subject to PAYE — business income as a sole proprietor, rental income from property, or substantial investment income. Someone who earns only a salary generally is not.

There is an exemption: an individual is not required to pay provisional tax if they do not carry on a business and their taxable income will not exceed the tax threshold for the year, or their income from interest, dividends, foreign dividends, rental from fixed property and remuneration from an unregistered employer will be R30 000 or less for the tax year.

Deceased estates are not provisional taxpayers.

What you need before you file

  • IRP5 or IT3(a) certificates from every employer during the year
  • IT3(b) and IT3(c) certificates for investment income and disposals
  • Your medical scheme tax certificate, plus receipts for out-of-pocket costs
  • Retirement annuity contribution certificates
  • A logbook, if you are claiming business travel
  • Section 18A certificates for donations to public benefit organisations
  • Rental income and expense records, if you let property
  • Records of any foreign income or assets

If you miss the deadline

File anyway, as soon as you can. SARS charges administrative non-compliance penalties that recur every month a return remains outstanding, charged per outstanding return, and interest accrues on any unpaid tax at 10.25% per annum. The penalty applies whether or not tax was owed — so a year in which you were due a refund can still generate penalties if the return was never filed.

Where there are reasonable grounds, a request for remission of penalties can be lodged. That works far better with a completed filing history behind it than with several years still outstanding.

Questions

Filing Season questions

When does Filing Season 2026 close?

Non-provisional individual taxpayers have until 23 October 2026. Provisional taxpayers and trusts have until 22 January 2027.

What is an auto-assessment?

SARS issues an auto-assessment using third-party data it already holds from employers, banks, medical schemes and retirement funds. Auto-assessment notices for 2026 were issued from 1 to 12 July 2026. If the assessment is correct and complete, no further action is needed.

Should I accept my auto-assessment?

Only if it captures everything. SARS does not know about deductions it has no third-party record of, such as home office expenses, business travel against a travel allowance, or out-of-pocket medical costs. If any of those apply, file a return rather than accepting the auto-assessment.

I have several years outstanding. Where do I start?

At the beginning. Returns are filed year by year, and each outstanding year is generating its own monthly penalty until it is submitted. Bringing several years current at once is routine work — get in touch and we will work out the sequence and what it will take.
