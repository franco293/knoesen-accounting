Compliance Calendar

The South African tax deadline calendar

Every recurring SARS, Department of Employment and Labour, and CIPC deadline that applies to a South African business, in one place.

Last reviewed 3 August 2026 against SARS and National Treasury sources.

Not every deadline below applies to every business. Which ones apply to you depends on your entity type, whether you are VAT-registered, and whether you have employees. If you are not sure, ask — it takes two minutes to work out and it is cheaper than a penalty.

Every Month

Monthly deadlines

Recurring monthly South African tax deadlines
DueWhatApplies to
7th EMP201 — monthly employer declaration and payment of PAYE, UIF and SDL Every registered employer
7th UIF declaration to the Department of Employment and Labour Every employer

Where a deadline falls on a weekend or public holiday, it moves earlier, to the last business day before it — not later. This catches people out constantly.

Twice A Year

Bi-annual deadlines

Bi-annual South African tax deadlines
PeriodWhatApplies to
Sep – Oct EMP501 interim reconciliation — covering the six months March to August Every employer
Apr – May EMP501 annual reconciliation — covering the full tax year, generating IRP5 and IT3(a) certificates Every employer
31 Aug First provisional tax (IRP6) — estimate and payment for the first half of the year of assessment Provisional taxpayers with a February year-end
Last business day of Feb Second provisional tax (IRP6) — estimate and payment for the full year of assessment Provisional taxpayers with a February year-end

Provisional tax dates move with your year-end

The 31 August and end-February dates above assume a February year-end, which covers all individuals and most small companies. A company with a different financial year-end has provisional deadlines six months and twelve months after the start of its year of assessment instead. SARS also allows an optional third "top-up" payment after year-end to reduce interest where the second estimate fell short.

The Cost Of Missing Them

What late actually costs

Late income tax returns

SARS levies administrative non-compliance penalties that recur every month a return remains outstanding, charged per outstanding return. The penalty applies whether or not any tax was owed for that year, which is why a business that made a loss and "didn't bother" can still accumulate a significant liability.

Late payment

Interest runs on late or underpaid tax at 10.25% per annum, applicable from 1 March 2026. Late payment of VAT attracts interest at the same rate, in addition to a percentage-based penalty on the amount.

Understated provisional tax

A second provisional estimate that falls short of the prescribed accuracy thresholds attracts an underestimation penalty. This is one of the few penalties that is entirely avoidable by doing the calculation properly rather than repeating last year's figure.

Late CIPC annual returns

There is no monthly penalty — instead, CIPC may start the deregistration process. The consequence is worse than a fine: a deregistered company loses its legal existence, its bank accounts can be frozen, and directors lose the protection of separate legal personality.
