Bookkeeping that stays current

Current records make VAT preparation, management reporting and year-end work easier. If the books have fallen behind, tell us which periods need attention so we can discuss catch-up work before starting a monthly routine.

We record your monthly transactions accurately and keep them up to date within the agreed scope: bank and credit card reconciliations, supplier and customer ledgers, cash and petty cash, and supporting documents. You supply the records and answer questions about transactions; we agree when this information is needed each month.

Management accounts you can use

Annual financial statements cover a completed financial year. Management accounts give you more frequent information about income, expenses, balances and cash flow to support decisions during the year.

We prepare management accounts at whatever interval genuinely helps — monthly for businesses with tight cash flow or bank covenants, quarterly for steadier operations — with a short plain-language note on what moved and why, rather than a stack of unexplained figures.

Questions

Accounting questions we get asked

What is the difference between an audit, a review and a compilation?

A compilation means the accountant prepares the annual financial statements from your records without expressing an assurance opinion. An independent review and an audit are assurance engagements with progressively more testing. The required engagement depends on the applicable law, the company's circumstances and its Memorandum of Incorporation. We provide compilations only; an audit or independent review requires a suitable assurance provider.

How often should bookkeeping be done?

Monthly, in almost every case. Monthly bookkeeping keeps VAT returns accurate, makes payroll reconciliations straightforward, and means year-end is a review rather than a scramble. It also means you can see how the business is actually performing while you can still do something about it.

Do you work with Xero, Sage, QuickBooks or spreadsheets?

Tell us your current software or spreadsheet setup so we can confirm how to work with it and whether any changes are needed for the agreed service.

My books are years behind. Is that a problem?

Tell us which periods are outstanding and what records are available. We can assess the reconstruction and filing work, agree its scope, and discuss a monthly routine going forward. Missing records may affect what can be completed and how long it takes.

How long must I keep my accounting records?

Generally seven years. The Companies Act requires company records to be kept for seven years, and the Tax Administration Act requires records supporting a return to be retained for five years from the date the return was submitted — longer if an audit, objection or appeal is in progress.
