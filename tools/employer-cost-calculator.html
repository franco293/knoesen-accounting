Three charges, three different shapes

The statutory add-ons to a salary do not scale the same way, which is why a flat "add 10%" rule of thumb misleads at both ends.

Employer UIF is 1% but capped on monthly earnings. On a R10 000 salary it is a real R100 a month; on a R60 000 salary it is still R177.12. As a proportion it shrinks to almost nothing at senior level.

The Skills Development Levy is 1% with no ceiling at all, but the whole business is exempt while its annual payroll stays under R500 000. That produces a genuine cliff: the hire that takes you past the threshold makes the levy payable on your entire payroll, not just on the new person. A business at R490 000 of payroll adding a R60 000 role does not pick up R600 of levy — it picks up R5 500.

COIDA is an annual assessment, not a monthly deduction, and the rate depends entirely on what your business does. Office-based work sits well under 1%; construction, transport and heavy industry run several times higher. There is also a minimum assessment per employer — roughly R1 621 for commercial employers — so a very small payroll pays that floor rather than the calculated amount.

Why this matters beyond budgeting

A candidate who names a take-home figure has not told you what the role costs. Between PAYE, UIF, SDL and COIDA, the gap between "what they want in their account" and "what leaves the business" is routinely a third or more. Our net to gross calculator handles the first half of that; this handles the second.

What this doesn't cover

  • The COIDA minimum assessment, which is an employer-level floor rather than a per-employee amount.
  • Bargaining council levies and industry-specific funds, which apply in construction, motor, road freight, hospitality and others and can be substantial.
  • Leave provisions, recruitment cost, equipment, training, and the employer's share of any fringe benefit — all real costs of a hire that are not statutory charges.
  • The employment tax incentive, which can reduce PAYE for qualifying young employees and would lower your net cost.
