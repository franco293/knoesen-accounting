Why this is worse than people expect

The administrative penalty is not a percentage of tax owed. It is a fixed rand amount charged for each outstanding return, for every month it stays outstanding, and it accrues whether or not you owe SARS a cent.

A nil return that is three years late still costs R9 000 at the lowest band. Someone with four years outstanding is not four times worse off than someone with one — they are far worse, because the oldest return has been accruing the longest.

The band is set by your preceding year's taxable income, not the income of the year the return covers. So a good year followed by a quiet one still attracts the bigger penalty.

The one thing that stops it

Filing. The penalty stops the month the return is submitted, and nothing else halts it — not paying, not phoning, not waiting. Every month spent deciding what to do costs another instalment.

Penalties already raised can be disputed through a Request for Remission, and SARS may remit them where there were exceptional circumstances or where it is a first infringement. That is a real avenue and it is worth using, but it works far better once the returns are actually in.

What this doesn't cover

  • Understatement penalties under Chapter 16, which run from 0% to 200% depending on behaviour and whether the disclosure was voluntary. Those are a different regime and are assessed case by case.
  • The provisional tax under-estimation penalty, which has its own rules — see the provisional tax calculator.
  • CIPC penalties, which are separate from SARS entirely — see the CIPC fee calculator.
  • Criminal sanction, which is possible for wilful non-compliance.

If you have returns outstanding, the number above is not a reason to panic — it is a reason to move.
